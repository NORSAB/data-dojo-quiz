## Lección 1: About the Databricks Certification Program

Databricks Certification exams are a great way to validate your data and AI skills on the Databricks Platform. To get you started, we'll cover some fundamental information about the program itself.

**Certification vs. Accreditation**

First, let's answer a question we hear all the time: "What is the difference between a Databricks Certification and a Databricks Accreditation?" Databricks offers two types of tests you can take to validate your skills and earn digital  shareable badges on social networks: Certifications and Accreditations. Here's the difference between the two:

Certification

A certification is a **proctored** exam completed through our online examination portal, Webassessor. These exams have stricter rules and requirements to complete, have an **associated cost**, and include a **retake policy** (you must wait 14 days between unsuccessful exam attempts).

Accreditation

Accreditations are free, **non-proctored** quizzes completed through the Databricks Academy. These have fewer rules and can be attempted as many times as needed (they do not have a re-take policy).

**Heading**

In this course, we'll strictly focus on available Databricks **certifications** and your role as a test taker in making sure you are successful in your certification attempt with Databricks.

**Certification Levels**

Certification exams are offered at either an "associate" or "professional" level, depending on the material being assessed and where it falls in one of our Databricks learning pathways. Please note that not all pathways offer both "associate" and "professional" levels. 

- **Associate level:** We expect **~6+ months** of work experience with Databricks.

- **Professional level:** We expect **~2 years **of work experience with Databricks. 

Once you earn your badge, you can share them on social media sites such as LinkedIn or Medium. Badges you have earned are available from credentials.databricks.com

## Lección 2: Registering for a Databricks Certification Exam

Databricks utilizes a third-party vendor to proctor certification exams. Our exams are proctored to ensure that testing is fair and consistent across all examinees. For this, Databricks uses Kryterion Webassessor, also referred to as Webassessor, a common vendor for exam proctoring in the technology training industry. Below, we include key information about Webassessor that you need to know as a Databricks certification exam candidate.

### **Webassessor Test Taker Account**

If you haven't already done so, you'll need to create a Webassessor Test Taker account for Databricks. Even if you've created a Webassessor account for another company that uses Webassessor as a proctoring service, you'll still need to create one for Databricks. 

Keep in mind that whatever email address you use for your Webassessor account will be linked to your certifications and badges, so be cautious of using a company email for this account in case you have plans of changing employers in the future.

### **Forgotten Password**

If you've forgotten your Webassessor account password, there is a link at the top of the webpage to reset your password. Note that Kryterion Webassessor is a third-party service and their help services are not connected to those available at Databricks.

### **Webassessor vs. Databricks Academy**

Be aware that your Test Taker Account is **not** connected to your Databricks Academy account. While Databricks accreditations are completed through Databricks Academy, Databricks **certification exams** are strictly proctored through Webassessor.

Register for Databricks Certification Exam

The following steps will guide you through how to create your Webassessor Test Taker account and register for proctored Databricks Certification Exams.

## Lección 3: What to Expect on Exam Day

In addition to coming to your Databricks certification exam with applicable knowledge about the Databricks product, there are other requirements we'd like to draw your attention to. In this lesson, we outline the requirements to keep in mind for your testing environment. Note that if your environment does not meet the requirements, the proctor may cancel your exam and you'll need to reschedule.

Computer Requirements

- Kryterion strongly recommends use of a non-work related or personal computer for online proctored (OLP) exams.

- Virtual machines are not permitted and should not be used to take an online exam. 

- In order to participate in an OLP exam, you must download and install the provided Secure Browser software. This may require Administrator privileges on your system. Launching the Secure Browser software will not work on it's own; refer to article: How to Launch Online Proctored(OLP) Exam

- For further Information on hardware and software requirements for the system please visit system requirements check

- Use this link to validate your system requirements as a prerequisite before the exam day  System Readiness Check

Room Requirements

- Choose a room that is well lit, quiet, and free from distraction including other people.
- Bring a valid government issued ID with you. The name on that ID must match the name on your Webassessor account.
- Your immediate surroundings are clutter-free. There is only one active computer, one active monitor, one keyboard, and one mouse.
- If you have a CCTV camera in your testing/room environment, make sure that it is not visible in the camera view, or that your screen is not visible/exposed to the CCTV.

- Cell phones are not permitted in the testing area.

Not only must your computer and your environment meet specific requirements to successfully take and complete your Databricks certification exam, but you must also be aware of your behavior during the course of the exam. Below is a non-exhaustive list of some behaviors you need to be aware of that may impact your ability to successfully complete your exam.

Reading your exam material aloud. If your voice and volume level are clear enough for your Proctor to understand, then you may be flagged.

Using any test aid (books, papers, writing instrument, phone) that Databricks did not previously approve for this exam.

Excessive background noise or other audio issues that interfere with the online proctoring process. You are responsible for providing a quiet, distraction-free environment prior to starting the exam.

Loss of video or poor video resolution that interferes with the online proctoring process will result in your exam being paused or suspended until troubleshooting is completed. This suspension is to ensure that exam content is being secured.

Leaving the test environment prior to the completion of the exam is not permitted and will result in the forfeiture of your exam. Please ensure all necessary arrangements and personal tasks have been taken care of prior to beginning the exam process.

Taking a picture of the exam content or having a recording device directed at the exam content will result in an exam suspension.

Please note that this is a non-exhaustive list of behaviors that could result in your exam session being terminated early or flagged for dispute. Webassessor and Databricks take the security of our exam materials seriously and want to ensure that no exam candidate has an unfair advantage over another. If you require special accommodations for taking a certification exam due to a disability or protected characteristic, please reach out to Databricks Help Center to discuss the available options for your unique case.

**AI Prep Guides**

The AI chatbot you already use (we use Genie Code) can be a sharp study partner for any Databricks certification if you use it well. The **AI Prep Guide** shows you how to prime it with the official exam guide, catch the outdated answers AI tends to give, find your weak spots, and pair it all with the hands-on practice every Databricks exam demands. Check out these steps below and try the AI Prep Guide yourself!

Introduction

These six steps turn your AI into a personal tutor for your exam. One honest caveat first: AI can be confidently wrong about Databricks products, and no tool guarantees a passing score. Always verify what it tells you against your official exam guide and docs.databricks.com.

Step 1 - Set up

Gather what you need: a booked exam date, your exam guide PDF, an AI tool you already use, a Databricks account (you can create a free Databricks Academy account) for hands-on practice, and a study journal to track weak spots and unfamiliar terms.

Step 2 - Prime your AI

Upload your exam guide and paste the priming prompt at the start of every session. It tells your AI to teach only current, official Databricks content, ground its answers in docs.databricks.com and Databricks Academy, and say so when it cannot confirm something.

Step 3 - Find your exam's "renamed product" traps

Most AI tools were trained before recent Databricks Platform changes, so they confidently use old product names. Use the prompt provided in the AI Prep Guide to catch the latest product names.

Step 4 - Run 5-step loop

Work through your exam guide one section at a time, pasting five prompts in order: Orient (a plain-English overview of the objectives), Diagnose (a 10-question quiz), Deep dive (teach the objectives you missed), Practice (a full mock exam), and Repair (explain the ones you got wrong).

For each section in your exam guide, paste these prompts into your AI in order:

5. Repair

Here are the questions I got wrong: [PASTE]. For each: explain my misconception, explain why the right answer is right, and give me a related question that tests the same concept differently.

Step 5 - Hands-on minimum

Almost every Databricks certification tests practical skills, not just recall. Have your AI turn the exam objectives into a short hands-on checklist, then build every item in your Databricks workspace. Any task you cannot complete is your next study target.

Step 6 - Suggested pace

Work backward from your exam date in two-week sprints. Diagnose every section early to find your weakest spots, deep-dive and build hands-on labs through the middle, then run full mock exams at the end to surface gaps and build stamina.
